# Physical Planet Model (Part 1) Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** Give every system a drawn age, and compute for every planet its
physical inputs, interior layers, radius, internal heat and a bulk type
class from its formation context, alongside the existing presets.

**Architecture:** A new module `crates/mapgen/src/planet/` holds the
model: inputs drawn from the formation context (never from a type name),
a layered interior with a compressed mass–radius relation, radiogenic and
tidal heat, and a classifier. In this part the model is computed on
demand from a stored system (`planet::body_model`) and is not stored;
the worlds keep their presets. The only output change is the system age,
which replaces the fixed 4.6 Gyr everywhere it is read.

**Tech Stack:** Rust (crate `mapgen`, CLI `crates/cli`), serde, rand,
ts-rs bindings.

**Spec:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-11-physical-planet-model-design.md`
(step 1 of its "Order of work"; derivations 1 and 7 of "Derived, in
order", restricted to what needs no atmosphere).

## Parts of track A

The spec's five steps become five plans, each written when the previous
one has landed, each leaving `main` working:

1. **This plan.** Inputs, interior and radius, internal heat, system age,
   bulk classifier. No stored format change.
2. Tectonic regime, core state and dynamo, atmosphere and escape, climate
   and surface liquid, life; the classifier gains the surface classes
   (ocean world, hydrocarbon world, runaway greenhouse, lava world). The
   spec's "Order of work" does not place the core and dynamo: they go
   here, with the regime, since both read the same cooling history and
   the atmosphere's escape reads the field.
3. Drawn systems from the formation context (mass function), archetypes
   replace presets, interim surface mapping, `WorldParams` change, format
   bumps. Decide there which of #312, #313, #315 go inside.
4. Large moons through the model (tidal heat from the host, subsurface
   oceans, splitting the water layer by state).
5. Form and API; the card showing the derived state and why.

## Global Constraints

- Scope: only what this plan lists. No atmosphere, regime, dynamo, life,
  archetype or form change.
- No format bump in this part: new stored fields (`age_gyr`) are
  additive, `#[serde(default = …)]`, and `SYSTEM_FORMAT_VERSION` (2) and
  `FORMAT_VERSION` stay as they are. Part 3 bumps them.
- Anti-goal "types in disguise": no function of `planet::` takes a type
  name (`body_type`, preset name, `PLANET_TYPES`) as input. Inputs come
  from `planet::Context` and the mass only.
- Every relation cites its source in its doc comment, in the style of
  `world.rs:126-132` (authors, year, doi or equation); every constant
  without a source says `Calibration, no source.` and names the bodies
  it reproduces. The constants given below are from memory: check each
  against its source before relying on it, and fix the value (not the
  test tolerance) when the source differs.
- Determinism: model draws use their own RNG,
  `StdRng::seed_from_u64(u64::from(body_seed(system.seed, "<id>/model")))`,
  and the age its own stream; no existing draw may shift.

- [ ] **Step 3: Implement `solid_interior`**

  The algorithm is a choice the tests do not fix:

```text
refractory = M (1 − water − envelope); iron = CMF · refractory; rock = refractory − iron
ρ_unc(refractory) = 1 / (CMF/ρ_Fe + (1 − CMF)/ρ_rock)     ρ_Fe = 7.0, ρ_rock = 3.344 g/cm³
R_unc = (refractory · ρ⊕ / ρ_unc)^(1/3)                     ρ⊕ = 5.513 (radii in R⊕, masses in M⊕)
R_zeng = (1.07 − 0.21 CMF) · refractory^(1/3.7)             Zeng, Sasselov & Jacobsen 2016
R_refr = min(R_zeng, R_unc)                                 small bodies are not compressed
k = (R_unc / R_refr)³ ≥ 1                                   compression of the refractory part
core radius = (iron · ρ⊕ / (ρ_Fe · k))^(1/3); rock layer up to R_refr
water shell: volume water · ρ⊕ / (ρ_water · k), ρ_water = 1.25 g/cm³
```

  `ρ_Fe = 7.0` (Fe–FeS alloy) and `ρ_water = 1.25` (mix of ice I and
  high-pressure ices) are calibrations reproducing Mercury and
  Ganymede–Callisto; `ρ_rock` reuses `UNCOMPRESSED_ROCK_DENSITY`
  (`world.rs:141`). Sharing the rock's compression with the water shell
  is a calibration checked against Zeng et al. 2019. Gravity is
  `M / R²`, density `ρ⊕ · M / R³`.

- [ ] **Step 3: Implement `draw_inputs`**

  Every rule below is a calibration (label it so) unless a source is
  named. `x = orbit_au / ice_line_au`, `N(μ, σ)` normal, `LN = 10^N(0, 0.3)`.

  - **Core mass fraction:** `N(0.325 + 0.15 · [Fe/H], 0.05)` clamped to
    0.15–0.55. If `x < 0.3`, with probability 0.05 the body is stripped
    by giant impacts: `U(0.55, 0.75)` (Mercury; super-Mercuries are a
    few percent of small planets, Adibekyan et al. 2021, check).
  - **Water:** `x ≥ 1`: `U(0.2, 0.5)`. `x < 1`: `10^U(−4, −2.7)` (Earth
    2.3e-4), except with probability 0.1 a body that migrated in from
    beyond the ice line, drawn as `x ≥ 1`.
  - **Envelope** (core accretion; Lee & Chiang 2016, Pollack et al.
    1996, check):
    - `M < 0.5`: 0 (formed after the gas disc dispersed).
    - `0.5 ≤ M < 10`: it formed in the gas era with probability
      `(M − 0.5) / 4.5` clamped to 0–1. If it did, `10^N(log10(0.02 · M/5), 0.5)`
      clamped to 0.3; otherwise 0.
    - `10 ≤ M < 30`: `U(0.05, 0.3)` (Uranus, Neptune 0.1–0.2).
    - `M ≥ 30`: `1 − M_core / M`, `M_core = min(U(5, 20), 0.1 M)`.
  - **Volatiles:**

    ```text
    refractory share r = 1 − water − envelope
    co2 = (1e-4 · r + 0.05 · water) · LN
    n2  = (2e-6 · r + 1e-3 · water) · LN
    s   = 1e-4 · r · LN
    ch4 = 0.01 · water · LN
    nh3 = 0.01 · water · LN
    ```

    Earth and Venus carbon (about 1e-4) and Earth's nitrogen (about
    2e-6); for the ice ratios, cite comet volatile ratios (e.g. Mumma &
    Charnley 2011) or label them as calibration.
  - **Radiogenic abundance:** `10^N(0, 0.15)` (Frank, Meyer & Mojzsis
    2014, check).

  Draw in a fixed order (CMF, stripping, water, migration, envelope,
  volatiles, radiogenic) and document it: part 3 adds the mass draw in
  front of it.

  `classify`, in this order (the thresholds are constants, each cited):

  1. `M ≥ GIANT_MIN_MASS_EARTH` and `envelope ≥ GIANT_MIN_ENVELOPE`:
     `GasGiant` if `envelope ≥ 0.5`, otherwise `IceGiant`. A `GasGiant`
     with `period_days ≤ 10` is `HotJupiter` (Wright et al. 2012).
  2. `envelope ≥ 1e-3` and `radius ≥ 1.6`: `MiniNeptune` (Rogers 2015:
     above 1.6 R⊕ most planets are not rocky).

  4. `radius ≥ 1.25`: `SuperEarth` (Borucki et al. 2011 size classes),
     else `Rocky`.
