# Intermediate-size planets (about 1.5 to 4 Earth radii): sub-Neptunes, mini-Neptunes, water worlds, Hycean worlds

Conventions: a link means the fact was seen in a search or abstract page during this research session. Items marked "(from memory, unverified)" are standard literature values not re-checked online; verify before publishing. Current as of 2026-11-15 (the K2-18b debate may have moved since the last items found).

## The radius valley (Fulton gap) and its explanations

### Takeaway (section 1) (section 1)

Close-in (P < 100 d) small planets show a deficit near 1.5-2.0 R_earth separating rocky "super-Earths" (< 1.5 R_earth) from gas-enveloped "sub-Neptunes" (2-3 R_earth). Photoevaporation and core-powered mass loss both carve it from initially gas-rich cores; they differ in energy source and in predicted trends, and the data do not yet cleanly pick one.

### Cited Findings (section 1) (section 1)

- Fulton et al. 2017 (California-Kepler Survey III, AJ, arXiv:1703.10375) report a factor of 2 or more deficit in occurrence at 1.5-2.0 R_earth, splitting P < 100 d planets into < 1.5 R_earth and about 2.0-3.0 R_earth groups; they read this as rocky cores of 1.5 R_earth or less with varying low-density gas — [arXiv abs](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.10375>)
- Van Eylen et al. 2018 (MNRAS, arXiv:1710.05398), using asteroseismic stellar radii, place the valley near 2 R_earth with super-Earths about 1.5 and sub-Neptunes about 2.5 R_earth; the valley slope is R ∝ P^γ with γ = -0.09 (+0.02/-0.04), negative as photoevaporation predicts and opposite to late gas-poor formation of rocky planets — [arXiv abs](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.05398>)
- Owen & Wu 2013 (ApJ 775, 105, arXiv:1303.3899, DOI 10.1088/0004-637X/775/2/105) predicted the evaporation-driven bimodal size distribution with a deficit near 2 R_earth, with most loss in the first ~100 Myr and about half of Kepler candidates significantly eroded — [ar5iv](<https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1303.3899>), [ADS](<https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013ApJ...775..105O/abstract>)
- Lopez & Fortney 2013 predicted the same feature independently (named in the Owen & Wu summary; the citation itself was not fetched) — [ar5iv summary page](<https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1303.3899>)
- Owen & Wu 2017 (ApJ 847, 29, arXiv:1705.10810), "The evaporation valley in the Kepler planets", compares evaporation models with the observed valley — [arXiv abs](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.10810>)
- Core-powered mass loss: Ginzburg, Schlichting & Sari 2018 (MNRAS 476, 759, arXiv:1708.01621), "Core-powered mass loss and the radius distribution of small exoplanets" — [arXiv abs](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.01621>)
- Gupta & Schlichting 2019 (MNRAS 487, 24, arXiv:1811.03202), "Sculpting the valley ... as a by-product of planet formation: the core-powered mass-loss mechanism" — [arXiv abs](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.03202>)
- Mechanism distinction (general knowledge, from memory, unverified here): photoevaporation is driven by stellar XUV and is strongest in the first ~100 Myr; core-powered mass loss is driven by the cooling core's bolometric luminosity and the planet's own thermal energy, and works over Myr to Gyr. The Bean, Raymond & Owen review (below) discusses both.
- Review: Bean, Raymond & Owen 2021, "The Nature and Origins of Sub-Neptune Size Planets", JGR Planets 126, e2020JE006639, DOI 10.1029/2020JE006639, arXiv:2010.11867; notes the frequency drop between about 1.5 and 2.0 R_earth implying two classes of small planets — [arXiv abs](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11867v1>), [PMC copy](<https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7900964>)
- A third alternative is gas-poor formation of rocky planets late in the disc (Lee & Chiang 2016 and followers, from memory, unverified); Van Eylen's negative slope disfavors it — [arXiv abs 1710.05398](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.05398>)

### Inferences (section 1) (section 1)

- The valley is best described as a transition in envelope retention at fixed core composition, not a break in core mass; whether the stripped cores are Earth-like rock is partly constrained by the valley position (Van Eylen: substantial rocky fraction).
- Whether a planet at 1.5-2 R_earth is a "stripped core" or a "water world" cannot be settled from radius alone.

### Gaps (section 1) (section 1)

- Direct head-to-head tests of photoevaporation vs core-powered mass loss (e.g. Rogers, Owen & Schlichting 2024 MNRAS, my memory of the title and venue) were not retrieved; also Fulton & Petigura 2018 (stellar-mass dependence). Not verified.

## Structure models and classes of intermediate planets

### Takeaway (section 2) (section 2)

Proposed classes: rocky core plus H/He envelope (gas dwarf / mini-Neptune), water world (up to about 50% water ice/fluid), miscible metal-rich envelope, Hycean (water ocean under H2 atmosphere), magma-ocean sub-Neptune, and steam world. Radius and mass alone are degenerate among them; atmospheric spectra are what is starting to separate them, with mixed results.

### Cited Findings (section 2) (section 2)

- Zeng et al. 2019 (PNAS 116, 9723, DOI 10.1073/pnas.1812905116, arXiv:1906.04253): growth-model Monte Carlo argues planets of 2-4 R_earth are largely water worlds with cores up to 50% ices; a roughly 10-15 M_earth half-ice, half-rock planet has R about 2.5 R_earth, matching the second peak; any 2-4 R_earth planet needs at most a few mass percent of gas — [arXiv](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.04253v1>), [secondary summary](<https://earthsky.org/space/exoplanet-water-worlds-deep-oceans-2019-study/>). Note the arXiv id given here comes from the search result; the id 1812.08166 that circulates in some memory is wrong (it is a particle-physics proceedings).
- Luque & Pallé 2022 (Science, arXiv:2209.03871): small transiting planets around M dwarfs fall into three populations by density: rocky, water-rich (consistent with about 50% rock, 50% water), and gas-rich; they argue density, not radius, separates rocky from water-rich, and the result is inconsistent with a simple evaporation bimodality; water worlds would form beyond the snow line and migrate in — [IAC page](<https://www.iac.es/en/science-and-technology/publications/density-not-radius-separates-rocky-and-water-rich-small-planets-orbiting-m-dwarf-stars>), [astrobiology.com summary](<https://astrobiology.com/2022/09/08/density-not-radius-separates-rocky-and-water-rich-small-planets-orbiting-m-dwarf-stars/>). Sample size differs across press reports (43 planets in 26 systems vs TESS-based wording); check the paper.
- Counter-evidence: Rogers, Dorn, Raj, Schlichting & Young (arXiv:2409.17394, ApJ 2025, DOI 10.3847/1538-4357/ad9f61), "Most super-Earths have less than 3% water", population-level 1-sigma upper limit of about 3% water mass fraction — [astrobiology.com listing](<https://astrobiology.com/2024/09/most-super-earths-have-less-than-3-water.html>), [arXiv listing via search](<https://searchworks.jioinstitute.edu.in/articles/edsarx__edsarx.2409.17394>). Also Rogers, Schlichting & Young 2024 (ApJ 970, 47, arXiv:2402.14072): hydrogen ingassed into the interior oxidises iron and makes about 0.5-1.0 wt% water endogenously; atmospheres stay H-dominated by mole fraction but steam-dominated by mass — [arXiv](<https://web3.arxiv.org/abs/2402.14072>)
- Possibly the same debate: "Conclusive evidence for a population of water worlds around M dwarfs remains elusive" (Rogers et al., ApJL 947 L19, 2023, from memory, unverified; one search of the user-style citation did not confirm it).
- Hycean worlds: Madhusudhan, Piette & Constantinou 2021 (ApJ 918, 1, arXiv:2108.10888, DOI 10.3847/1538-4357/abfd9c) define water-rich interiors with massive oceans under H2-rich atmospheres; radii up to 2.6 R_earth for 10 M_earth; wider habitable zone, inner edge near T_eq about 500 K for late M dwarfs; "Dark" and "Cold" Hycean variants — [ADS](<https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021ApJ...918....1M/abstract>), [astrobiology.com summary](<https://astrobiology.com/2021/08/25/habitability-and-biosignatures-of-hycean-worlds/>). The id 2108.10936 in some memory is wrong (a math paper).
- Magma-ocean sub-Neptunes and chemistry: Schlichting & Young 2022 (PSJ, DOI 10.3847/PSJ/ac68e6, arXiv:2107.10405): equilibrium model of atmosphere, silicate melt and iron core; over 93% of H sits in the atmosphere; H and O significant in metal cores; mantle water about 0.01-0.1 wt%; water-dominated atmospheres appear inconsistent with observations — [arXiv](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.10405>)
- Shorttle et al. 2024 (ApJL 962, L8, DOI 10.3847/2041-8213/ad206e, arXiv:2401.05864): a magma-ocean model reproduces K2-18b's JWST spectrum to about 3 sigma, as credible as a water ocean; CO2/CO above 4 microns could discriminate — [arXiv](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05864>), [astrobiology.com](<https://astrobiology.com/2024/01/12/distinguishing-oceans-of-water-from-magma-on-mini-neptune-k2-18b/>)
- Miscible-envelope sub-Neptune: Benneke et al. 2024 (arXiv:2403.03325), H2/He mixed with high-molecular-weight volatiles, mean molecular weight 5.47 for TOI-270 d — [arXiv](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03325>)
- Supercritical / steam worlds: see GJ 9827 d below (water above 31% by volume).
- Hydrostatic H/He envelope plus core mass-radius relations (Lopez & Fortney 2014; Weiss & Marcy 2014; Chen & Kipping 2017; Zeng, Sasselov & Jacobsen 2016) — from memory, not re-verified.

### Inferences (section 2) (section 2)

- A sensible categorical scheme for generation: (1) rocky super-Earth, (2) gas dwarf sub-Neptune (few percent H/He by mass on a rocky/iron core), (3) water-rich/steam world (tens of percent water), (4) Hycean candidate (water ocean plus H2 envelope, cool), (5) magma-ocean mini-Neptune. Class boundaries are model-dependent and densities overlap.
- Terminology: "mini-Neptune" and "sub-Neptune" are used interchangeably in the literature; "gas dwarf" is used for rocky core plus thin H/He.

### Gaps (section 2) (section 2)

- Exact bulk-composition ternary boundaries (rock/water/H-He) were not extracted; Zeng 2019 and Luque & Pallé figures should be read directly.
- No peer-reviewed synthesis of "which class dominates" exists; the water-world fraction is unsettled (Rogers 2025 vs Luque & Pallé 2022).

## JWST results on individual intermediate planets

### Takeaway (section 3) (section 3)

JWST shows metal-rich or high-mean-molecular-weight atmospheres on several sub-Neptunes (GJ 1214b, TOI-270 d, GJ 9827 d), a likely volatile-rich interior for K2-18b, and a flat/high-MMW spectrum for LHS 1140b. The DMS biosignature claim for K2-18b is not statistically secure.

### Cited Findings (section 3) (section 3)

- Planet parameters, K2-18b (R about 2.6 R_earth, M about 8.6 M_earth, P about 33 d), TOI-270 d (R about 2.2, M about 4.8 M_earth), GJ 1214b (R about 2.7, M about 8.2 M_earth), LHS 1140b (R about 1.73, M about 5.6 M_earth): from memory, unverified. GJ 9827 d R = 1.98 R_earth is confirmed in the paper abstract below.
- K2-18b: Madhusudhan et al. 2023 (ApJL, arXiv:2309.05566) report CH4 (5 sigma) and CO2 (3 sigma) at about 1% each in an H2-rich atmosphere, no NH3, and a possible DMS signal — [arXiv](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.05566>)
- K2-18b: Madhusudhan et al. 2025 (ApJL 983, L40, DOI 10.3847/2041-8213/adc1c8, arXiv:2504.12267) MIRI LRS: 3 sigma evidence for DMS and/or DMDS, at least one at about 10 ppmv or more — [arXiv](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.12267v1>)
- Rebuttals: Welbanks et al. and Taylor et al. questioned the significance (named in coverage; paper ids not retrieved); a joint reanalysis of NIRISS, NIRSpec and MIRI (attributed to Piaulet-Ghorayeb et al. by a search summary; A&A DOI 10.1051/0004-6361/202555580, arXiv:2505.13407) finds insufficient evidence for DMS or DMDS, and about 25 more MIRI transits are needed for a 3 sigma test — [arXiv](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.13407>), [A&A](<https://www.aanda.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202555580>), [Space.com summary](<https://www.space.com/space-exploration/search-for-life/doubts-mount-further-over-signs-of-alien-life-on-k2-18b-this-is-evidence-of-the-scientific-process-at-work>). Note: the lead-author attribution of 2505.13407 and the A&A DOI pairing come from search snippets; verify.
- Pica-Ciamarra et al. (arXiv:2505.10539, v2 revised April 2026): only DMS consistently reaches ln B of 2.0 or more across datasets considered independently, but depends on detector offsets — [arXiv](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.10539>)
- Hu et al. 2025 (arXiv:2507.12622, submitted to AAS journals; publication status unconfirmed): four new NIRSpec transits confirm CH4 and CO2, infer a water-rich interior, only marginal DMS, abiotic photochemical routes — [arXiv](<https://web3.arxiv.org/pdf/2507.12622>), [astrobiology.com](<https://astrobiology.com/2025/07/18/a-water-rich-interior-in-the-temperate-sub-neptune-k2-18-b-revealed-by-jwst/>)
- Critical review: arXiv:2508.05961, "K2-18b does not meet the standards of evidence for life" (authors not extracted) — [arXiv](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.05961>)
- Madhusudhan's reply to critics: "nothing in this paper worries me" (quoted in coverage, a press quote, not a paper) — [Space.com](<https://www.space.com/space-exploration/search-for-life/doubts-mount-further-over-signs-of-alien-life-on-k2-18b-this-is-evidence-of-the-scientific-process-at-work>)
- TOI-270 d: Benneke et al. 2024 (arXiv:2403.03325): NIRISS/SOSS plus NIRSpec G395H 0.6-5.2 microns; CH4 9.4 sigma, CO2 4.8 sigma, H2O 2.5 sigma, possible SO2 and CS2; mean molecular weight 5.47; "miscible-envelope sub-Neptune". Holmberg & Madhusudhan 2024 read it as a possible Hycean world. Felix et al. 2025 (A&A 701, A296, DOI 10.1051/0004-6361/202555194) reanalysis: two interpretations (high-metallicity miscible envelope vs lower-metallicity Hycean) remain in conflict — [arXiv](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03325>), [A&A](<https://www.aanda.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202555194>), [search summary](<https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13039v2>)
- GJ 9827 d: Piaulet-Ghorayeb et al. 2024 (ApJL 974, L10, arXiv:2410.03527): NIRISS/SOSS 0.6-2.8 microns plus HST/WFC3, R = 1.98 R_earth, water background gas at volume mixing ratio above 31%, O/H about 4 by mass, no escaping metastable He; "steam world" — [arXiv](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03527>). Press says nearly pure water vapor; paper says above 31% only.
- GJ 1214b: Kempton et al. 2023 (Nature 620, 67, DOI 10.1038/s41586-023-06159-5): MIRI phase curve, Bond albedo about 0.51, metal-rich atmosphere under reflective clouds/haze, water likely — [Ideas/RePEc record](<https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/nature/v620y2023i7972d10.1038_s41586-023-06159-5.html>), [Oxford news](<https://www.physics.ox.ac.uk/news/mysterious-planet-seen-new-light>). No arXiv id found; also Gao et al. 2023 and Schlawin et al. 2024 (GJ 1214b transmission, from memory, unverified).
- LHS 1140 b: Cadieux et al. 2024 (ApJL 970, L2, arXiv:2406.15136): two NIRISS transits; H2-rich atmosphere rejected at above 10 sigma; flat spectrum fits airless or high-MMW; tentative N2 (2.3 sigma); faculae 5.8 sigma; water fraction possibly about 9-19% of mass per press — [arXiv](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.15136>), [summary](<https://astrobiology.com/2024/06/24/transmission-spectroscopy-of-the-habitable-zone-exoplanet-lhs-1140-b-with-jwst-niriss/>). LHS 1140 b is at the small end (R about 1.7), so it is a border case with super-Earths.
- JWST overview: "Highlights from Exoplanet Observations by JWST" (arXiv:2505.20520) — [arXiv](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.20520>)

### Inferences (section 3) (section 3)

- High-MMW or metal-rich atmospheres dominate the JWST sample of sub-Neptunes below about 3 R_earth; pure primordial H/He is the exception, not the rule.
- For K2-18b, the Hycean, magma-ocean, miscible-envelope and gas-dwarf interpretations all remain on the table; nothing at the time of the latest sources found here establishes a liquid ocean or biosignature.

### Gaps (section 3) (section 3)

- Late 2025 to 2026 developments after the sources above (new K2-18b transits, peer-reviewed publication of Hu et al., Welbanks and Taylor papers' ids) were not found.
- Mass and radius values for the examples were not verified online, except GJ 9827 d radius.

## Occurrence rates

### Takeaway (section 4) (section 4)

Planets of about 1-4 R_earth on P < 100 d orbits are the most common planet type found around Sun-like stars in Kepler data; the Fulton gap splits them into two sub-populations.

### Cited Findings (section 4) (section 4)

- Howard et al. 2012 (ApJS, "Planet occurrence within 0.25 AU of solar-type stars from Kepler", arXiv:1103.2541): arXiv id from search snippet; results not retrieved here — [search pointer](<https://physicstoday.aip.org/news/earth-size-exoplanets-in-comfortable-orbits-are-common>)
- Petigura, Howard & Marcy 2013 (PNAS, DOI 10.1073/pnas.1319909110, arXiv:1311.6806 from memory): about 11 +/- 4% of Sun-like stars have an Earth-size planet receiving 1-4 times Earth's stellar flux (a separate 22% figure uses a different HZ definition) — [PMC](<https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3845182>)
- Fressin et al. 2013 (ApJ 766, 81, arXiv:1301.0842): false positive rate and occurrence, from memory, unverified; not found in search.
- Fulton et al. 2017 gives the first clean occurrence-vs-radius gap — [arXiv](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.10375>)
- Review of demographics: arXiv:2409.13062, "Exoplanet Demographics: Physical and Orbital Properties" — [arXiv HTML](<https://arxiv.org/html/2409.13062v1>)

### Inferences (section 4) (section 4)

- Statement "sub-Neptunes are the most common planets around Sun-like stars" is consistent with Howard 2012, Fressin 2013 and Fulton 2017, but the exact percentages per star were not extracted here, so quote them only after checking the papers (roughly tens of percent of stars host such a planet at P < 100 d: from memory).

### Gaps (section 4) (section 4)

- Per-star occurrence numbers and the M-dwarf versus FGK comparison (Dressing & Charbonneau 2015; Hsu 2019) were not retrieved.

## Neptune desert, ridge and savanna

### Takeaway (section 5) (section 5)

Hot Neptunes are rare on P of about 3 days or less (desert); there is a ridge of enhanced occurrence at about 3.2-5.7 d and a sparsely but moderately populated savanna beyond. These features concern Neptune-size planets (about 3-8 R_earth), mostly above the sub-Neptune range of this note, but they bound it.

### Cited Findings (section 5) (section 5)

- Mazeh, Holczer & Faigler 2016 (A&A, arXiv:1602.07843, DOI not retrieved): desert in period-mass and period-radius planes; upper edge with M inversely proportional to P; lower edge roughly linear in P; less clear in period-radius — [arXiv](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.07843v2>), [A&A](<https://www.aanda.org/articles/aa/abs/2016/05/aa28065-15/aa28065-15.html>)
- Castro-Gonzalez et al. 2024 (A&A 689, A250, DOI 10.1051/0004-6361/202450957, arXiv:2409.10517): Kepler DR25 sample corrected for transit and detection probability; overdensity at about 3.2-5.7 d, 4.7 sigma above the desert and 3.5 sigma above the savanna; interpreted as late high-eccentricity tidal migration surviving photoevaporation — [arXiv](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.10517>), [ADS](<https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024A%26A...689A.250C/abstract>)
- Counterpoint: arXiv:2411.16960 argues about 87% of planets in the Mazeh period-radius desert do not lie in an irradiation-defined desert in the flux-radius plane — [arXiv](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.16960>)
- Szabo & Kiss 2011 coined the "sub-Jupiter desert" (named in Mazeh 2016 summary) — [arXiv listing](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.07843v2>)

### Inferences (section 5) (section 5)

- The desert is likely an evaporation plus migration boundary, so it overlaps physically with the valley mechanism: both depend on envelope loss at high irradiation.

### Gaps (section 5) (section 5)

- The "savanna" and "ridge" period boundaries come from one paper; replication with TESS samples not checked.
