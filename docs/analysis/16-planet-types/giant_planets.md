## 1. Gas giants vs ice giants (Uranus/Neptune; Helled reviews)

### Takeaway (section 1)

Ice giants (Uranus, Neptune) are defined by a heavy-element-dominated interior (water, ammonia, methane "ices" plus rock) with only a thin H/He envelope, whereas gas giants (Jupiter, Saturn) are H/He-dominated by mass. The "ice" label is historical: the interiors are probably hot fluid mixtures, and their exact composition and structure (layered vs. gradual) remain debated.

### Cited Findings (section 1)

- Helled, Nettelmann and Guillot (2020), "Uranus and Neptune: Origin, Evolution and Internal Structure", Space Science Reviews 216, 38, DOI 10.1007/s11214-020-00660-3, arXiv:1909.04891. It reviews composition, interior structure, formation (including possible giant impacts) and thermal evolution, and lists open questions, including interiors that may be non-adiabatic or inhomogeneous. — [arXiv 1909.04891](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.04891>); [DLR record](<https://elib.dlr.de/138504/>)
- Companion and newer reviews: Helled and Fortney (2020), "The Interiors of Uranus and Neptune: Current Understanding and Open Questions", arXiv:2007.10783; Helled (2025) review on the same planets, arXiv:2504.18219. — [arXiv 2007.10783](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.10783>); [arXiv 2504.18219](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.18219v1>)
- Chen and Kipping (2017) place Neptunian worlds as a distinct class of their mass-radius model (Terran, Neptunian, Jovian, stellar). — [arXiv 1603.08614](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.08614>)

### Inferences (section 1)

- For a catalogue, "ice giant" is best defined by bulk composition (heavy elements dominate the mass, H/He envelope of a few Earth masses to a few tens of percent) and a mass of roughly 10 to 30 Earth masses. The exoplanet analogue is the "Neptunian" class and the sub-Saturn/warm-Neptune range, but exoplanet bulk composition is rarely constrained.

### Gaps (section 1)

- Recall-based leads (unverified, no URL): Uranus about 14.5 Earth masses, Neptune about 17.1, mean densities about 1.27 and 1.64 g/cm3; Jupiter 1.33 and Saturn 0.69 g/cm3; Helled, Anderson, Podolak and Schubert (2011), ApJ 726, 15, "Interior models of Uranus and Neptune"; Fortney and Nettelmann (2010) Space Sci. Rev. 152, 423 on giant-planet interiors; superionic water results (Bethkenhagen et al. 2017). A Jupiter/Saturn H/He mass fraction table was not retrieved.
- Current (2024-2026) Uranus/Neptune JWST or Juno-era results were not searched.

## 2. Hot, ultra-hot, warm, cold, eccentric Jupiters; hot/warm Neptunes; hot Saturns

### Takeaway (section 2)

Classes are defined mainly by orbital period or equilibrium temperature. Hot Jupiters (P below about 10 d) are rare (about 1 percent of Sun-like stars) and have no single origin: disk migration and high-eccentricity tidal migration both seem to contribute. Ultra-hot Jupiters (dayside above about 2200-2500 K) are chemically distinct: H2, H2O, TiO and VO dissociate, metals ionize, and H- opacity dominates.

### Cited Findings (section 2)

- Dawson and Johnson (2018), "Origins of Hot Jupiters", Annual Review of Astronomy and Astrophysics 56, 175-221, arXiv:1801.06117. Three hypotheses (in situ formation, disk migration, high-eccentricity tidal migration); no single channel explains all evidence, and two major channels together can plausibly account for the properties. Hot Jupiters host about 1 percent of Sun-like stars. — [arXiv 1801.06117](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/1801.06117>); [PSU record](<https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/origins-of-hot-jupiters/>)
- Parmentier et al. (2018), "From thermal dissociation to condensation in the atmospheres of ultra hot Jupiters: WASP-121b in context", A&A 617, A110, DOI 10.1051/0004-6361/201833059, arXiv:1805.00096. SPARC/MITgcm models of four ultra-hot Jupiters: H2O, TiO, VO and H2 are dissociated and alkalis ionized on the dayside, CO survives; the vertical composition gradient weakens H2O features (the 4.5 micron CO feature is unchanged), a qualitative explanation for weak 1-2 micron water features. The model does not quantitatively match WASP-121b; WASP-121b, WASP-33b and Kepler-33Ab are noted as outliers. — [arXiv 1805.00096](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/1805.00096>); [A&A](<https://www.aanda.org/articles/aa/abs/2018/09/aa33059-18/aa33059-18.html>)
- Arcangeli et al. (2018), "H- Opacity and Water Dissociation in the Dayside Atmosphere of the Very Hot Gas Giant WASP-18 b", ApJL 855, L30, arXiv:1801.02489. Five HST/WFC3 eclipses (1.1-1.7 micron) plus Spitzer: H- opacity, thermal dissociation of water and ionization of metals shape the spectrum; solar metallicity [M/H] = -0.01 +/- 0.35 and C/O < 0.85 (3 sigma); supports thermal inversions in very hot gas giants. — [arXiv 1801.02489](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/1801.02489>)
- Gaudi et al. (2017), "A giant planet undergoing extreme-ultraviolet irradiation by its hot massive-star host" (KELT-9b), Nature 546, 514-518, DOI 10.1038/nature22392, arXiv:1706.06723. Host HD 195689 about 10,170 K; P about 1.48 d; dayside about 4600 K; about 700 times the EUV flux of WASP-33b. — [arXiv 1706.06723](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.06723>)
- Hot-Neptune desert: Armstrong et al. (2020) found TOI-849b, a planet smaller than Neptune but with anomalously high mass (about 40 Earth masses; sources differ, 39.1 vs 40.8) and Earth-like density, inside the hot-Neptune desert; envelope at most about 4 percent of mass. — [arXiv 2003.10314](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.10314v2>); [Space.com](<https://www.space.com/weird-alien-planet-gas-giant-core.html>)
- Hu, Seager and Yung (2015), ApJ 807, 8, arXiv:1505.02221: helium-dominated atmospheres on warm Neptunes/sub-Neptunes via hydrodynamic escape, proposed for GJ 436b. — [arXiv 1505.02221](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.02221v1>)

### Inferences (section 2)

- A usable parameter scheme for a generator: hot P < 10 d; warm 10-200 d; cold beyond that (the exact cuts vary by author; see Gaps). Ultra-hot is better keyed to Teq (above about 2200 K) than to P, since host spectral type matters (KELT-9b has P about 1.5 d but Teq far above WASP-18b).
- Neptune-mass planets at short period are scarce (desert), so "hot Neptune" objects are special cases such as TOI-849b.

## 3. Inflated planets and super-puffs; radius-inflation mechanisms

### Takeaway (section 3)

Hot Jupiters above about 1000-1500 K Teq are systematically larger than cooling models predict; the empirical fraction of incident flux deposited in the interior peaks near 1500 K then falls, which fits Ohmic dissipation. Super-puffs are a distinct low-mass, very-low-density class (Kepler-51), whose featureless spectra point to high-altitude haze (or, speculatively, rings).

### Cited Findings (section 3)

- Thorngren and Fortney (2018), "Bayesian Analysis of Hot Jupiter Radius Anomalies: Evidence for Ohmic Dissipation?", AJ 155, 214, DOI 10.3847/1538-3881/aaba13, arXiv:1709.04539. Inflation efficiency peaks at about 2.5 percent near Teq about 1500 K, then declines to about 0.2 percent at about 2500 K; the high-flux drop-off was predicted by Ohmic dissipation and not by other models; thermal tides predict far more radius variance than observed. — [arXiv 1709.04539](<https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1709.04539>); [arXiv abs](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.04539v1>)
- Masuda (2014), ApJ 783, 53, arXiv:1401.2885: TTV masses for Kepler-51 b, c and candidate d: about 2.1 (+1.5/-0.8), 4.0 +/- 0.4 and 7.6 +/- 1.1 Earth masses, densities around 0.05 g/cm3; periods 45.2, 85.3, 130.2 d near a 1:2:3 chain. — [arXiv 1401.2885](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.2885v2>)
- Libby-Roberts et al. (2020), "The Featureless Transmission Spectra of Two Super-puff Planets" (Kepler-51 b and d), AJ 159, 57, arXiv:1910.12988: HST/WFC3 1.15-1.63 micron spectra featureless (variations > 0.6 scale heights excluded), interpreted as a high-altitude aerosol layer at P < 3 mbar; erroneous masses ruled out. — [arXiv 1910.12988](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.12988>); [ADS](<https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020AJ....159...57L>)
- JWST NIRSpec/PRISM spectrum of Kepler-51d (arXiv:2505.21358): near-featureless, strongly sloped 0.6-5.3 micron transmission spectrum; hazes and a thin ring system weighed as explanations. — [arXiv 2505.21358](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.21358>)
- WASP-107b (JWST): both Sing et al. and Welbanks et al. (Nature, May 2024) found methane strongly depleted (Sing: 1.0 +/- 0.5 ppm, about 3 orders of magnitude below equilibrium), attributed to a hot interior with vigorous mixing, possibly from tidal heating; Batygin (2025, ApJ, arXiv:2505.01581) argues tidal dissipation needs Q about 100 and that Ohmic heating readily explains the inflated radius and high internal entropy (an alternative, not settled). — [arXiv 2505.01581](<https://arxiv.org/html/2505.01581v1>); [astrobiology.com summary](<https://astrobiology.com/2024/05/20/webb-studies-inflated-exoplanet-wasp-107-b/>)

### Inferences (section 3)

- Puffy hot Jupiters (high mass, Teq > 1000 K) and super-puffs (a few to tens of Earth masses, density below 0.1 g/cm3, cool Teq) are different phenomena and should be separate classes. WASP-107b is an intermediate "puffy Neptune/Saturn" requiring internal heating.

### Gaps (section 3)

- Recall-based leads (unverified, no URL): Guillot and Showman (2002), A&A 385, 156 (flux-driven inflation); Batygin and Stevenson (2010), ApJL 714, L238 (Ohmic); Demory and Seager (2011), ApJS 197, 12 (flux threshold); Tremblin et al. (2017) advection; Lopez and Fortney (2016) re-inflation; Thorngren et al. (2016), ApJ 831, 64 (heavy-element mass vs planet mass); Lee and Chiang (2016) super-puff formation; Wang and Dai (2019) hazes; Millholland (2019) tidal heating; Piro and Vissapragada (2020) rings. ## 4. Sudarsky classes I-V

### Takeaway (section 4)

The Sudarsky scheme classes giant planets by Teq and dominant cloud/absorber (I ammonia clouds; II water clouds; III cloudless; IV alkali-metal absorption; V silicate/iron clouds). It was built for reflected-light albedo and spectra predictions, and it is only loosely used today; modern literature uses Teq, pressure-temperature profile, and (for hot planets) the pM/pL absorber scheme and dissociation/ionization regimes.

### Cited Findings (section 4)

- Sudarsky, Burrows and Hubeny (2003), "Theoretical Spectra and Atmospheres of Extrasolar Giant Planets", ApJ 588, 1121, DOI 10.1086/374331, arXiv:astro-ph/0210216. Phase-averaged spectra of known planets vs stellar type, orbital distance, clouds, planet mass and gravity; planet-to-star flux ratios and direct-detection techniques. — [arXiv astro-ph/0210216](<https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0210216>)
- The 2000 albedo paper (Sudarsky, Burrows and Pinto 2000, ApJ 538, 885) is described secondhand; the 2003 paper says it bracketed albedo and reflection spectra with isolated and near-isothermal profiles; HD 209458b optical albedo about 3.8-4.5 percent is consistent with its alkali-dominated prediction. — [Burrows, Budaj and Hubeny via TA3](<https://www.ta3.sk/~budaj/ftp/paper/2008.apj678.1436/2008.apj678.1436.pdf>)

### Inferences (section 4)

- Class I (below about 150 K), II (about 150-250 K), III (roughly 250-800 K), IV (about 900-1500 K) and V (above about 1500 K) temperature ranges are recall-based and approximate; treat as flagged. Limits: ignores metallicity, C/O, internal heat, vertical mixing, photochemistry, thermal inversions, and 3D transport; JWST shows cloud/haze and disequilibrium chemistry (section 7) that the classes do not predict.

### Gaps (section 4)

- Exact class boundaries and the 2000 paper's DOI/arXiv id were not retrieved. Whether the classes are used in recent literature was not systematically checked (impression: mostly cited historically and in reflected-light/direct-imaging studies).

## 5. Chthonian planets and helium planets

### Takeaway (section 5)

Chthonian planets are the bare cores of hot giants stripped by evaporation; TOI-849b is the best candidate, but its core-remnant interpretation is contested. Helium planets are proposed as hydrodynamic escape outcomes for small, warm H/He planets, not for massive giants.

### Cited Findings (section 5)

- TOI-849b is described as the remnant core of a giant planet; the authors give two paths (stripped giant, or a core that failed to accrete gas); an outside researcher called the core interpretation "a little premature". — [arXiv 2003.10314](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.10314v2>); [Space.com](<https://www.space.com/weird-alien-planet-gas-giant-core.html>)
- Hu, Seager and Yung (2015): helium-dominated atmospheres from hydrodynamic escape on warm Neptune/sub-Neptune planets (GJ 436b as test case); NASA/JPL press text gives a timescale of up to about 10 Gyr (press release, not the paper). — [arXiv 1505.02221](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.02221v1>); [JPL](<https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/helium-shrouded-planets-may-be-common-in-our-galaxy/>)

### Inferences (section 5)

- The task text attributes TOI-849b to Mocquet 2014; that attribution looks wrong. TOI-849b was announced by Armstrong et al. 2020 (Nature 583, 39-42); Mocquet's work, if the paper I recall is right, is a theory paper on dense remnant planets.

### Gaps (section 5)

- Recall-based leads (unverified): Hebrard, Lecavelier des Etangs et al. (2004), ASP Conf. Ser. 321, 203 and Lecavelier des Etangs et al. (2004), A&A 418, L1 (evaporation and the "chthonian" term; the task's "Hebrard 2003" probably refers to this work); Mocquet, Grasset and Sotin (2014), Phil. Trans. R. Soc. ## 6. Upper mass boundary, brown dwarfs, directly imaged giants

### Takeaway (section 6)

There is no consensus line: the deuterium-burning limit (about 13 M_J) is a convenient observational convention, while formation-based arguments place the core-accretion ceiling near 4-10 M_J and argue the populations overlap.

### Cited Findings (section 6)

- Chabrier, Johansen, Janson and Rafikov (2014), "Giant planet and brown dwarf formation", Protostars and Planets VI, arXiv:1401.7559. Deuterium burning plays no role in either formation; the mass domains overlap, so there is no mass limit if objects are classified by formation. (PPVI title: "Formation of Brown Dwarfs vs Giant Planets: Confronting Theory with Observations".) — [arXiv 1401.7559](<https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1401.7559>); [PPVI page](<https://www2.mpia-hd.mpg.de/homes/ppvi/28chabrier.php>)
- Schlaufman (2018), "Evidence of an Upper Bound on the Masses of Planets and Its Implications for Giant Planet Formation", ApJ 853, 37, DOI 10.3847/1538-4357/aa961c, arXiv:1801.06185. 146 objects around solar-type stars: those up to about 4 M_J favour metal-rich hosts (core accretion), those above about 10 M_J do not; the occurrence minimum implies a core-accretion maximum below about 10 M_J; bodies above that around solar-type stars are likely brown dwarfs from gravitational instability. — [arXiv 1801.06185](<https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1801.06185>); [ADS](<https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018ApJ...853...37S/abstract>)
- Chen and Kipping (2017): four classes (Terran, Neptunian, Jovian, stellar), calibrated on 316 objects; Forecaster code. — [arXiv 1603.08614](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.08614>)

### Inferences (section 6)

- A generator can reasonably cap giants at about 13 M_J (convention) and tag 4-13 M_J as "super-Jupiter / borderline".

### Gaps (section 6)

- Recall-based leads (unverified): Spiegel, Burrows and Milsom (2011), ApJ 727, 57 (deuterium burning from about 11-16 M_J depending on composition); IAU 2003 working definition (13 M_J) and the 2018 IAU working-group update; Lecavelier des Etangs and Lissauer (2022), New Astron. Rev. 94, 101641; Marois et al. (2008) Science (HR 8799 b-d, about 7-10 M_J), Marois et al. (2010) Nature (HR 8799 e); Macintosh et al. (2015) Science (51 Eri b, about 2 M_J); Lagrange et al. (2010) and Snellen and Brown (2018) (beta Pic b, about 9-13 M_J); Bowler (2016) PASP review of imaged companions. No numbers for these objects were verified.

## 7. Mass-radius behaviour of giants

### Takeaway (section 7)

Giants occupy a degenerate-pressure plateau: radius is nearly independent of mass from a few tenths of M_J up to the stellar boundary.

### Cited Findings (section 7)

- Chen and Kipping (2017) find a transition near 2.0 Earth masses (Terran to Neptunian) and model Jovian worlds separately up to the stellar regime. — [arXiv 1603.08614](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.08614>)
- Thorngren and Fortney (2018) show radius anomalies depend on flux, so scatter at fixed mass is large for hot planets. — [arXiv 1709.04539](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.04539v1>)

### Inferences (section 7)

- Plateau width (about 0.3 to about 80 M_J near 1 R_J) matches the task brief but was not verified in a source this session.

### Gaps (section 7)

- Recall-based leads (unverified): Chen and Kipping slopes (Neptunian R roughly M^0.59, Jovian roughly M^-0.04, break near 0.41 M_J, about 131 Earth masses) and Jovian-to-stellar break near 0.08 solar masses; Hatzes and Rauer (2015), ApJL 810, L25 (giant planets defined by the mass-density relation, from about 0.3 M_J); Bashi et al. (2017), A&A 604, A83; Fortney, Marley and Barnes (2007), ApJ 659, 1661 (radius models). Exact numbers need checking.

## 8. JWST results that affect classification

### Takeaway (section 8)

JWST shows that bulk class (mass/radius) does not predict atmosphere: WASP-39b has photochemistry-driven SO2 and enhanced metallicity, and WASP-107b has a hot interior and depleted methane, making "equilibrium chemistry" classes unreliable.

### Cited Findings (section 8)

- Tsai et al. (2023), "Photochemically produced SO2 in the atmosphere of WASP-39b", Nature 617, 483-487, DOI 10.1038/s41586-023-05902-2. JWST ERS detected a 4.05 micron feature attributed to SO2; H2S destruction and sulfur oxidation (photochemistry) is the most plausible route; several 1D photochemical models reproduce it (NIRSpec PRISM and G395H, about 2.7 and 4.5 sigma); inferred metallicity about 10 times solar. The preprint was titled "Direct evidence of photochemistry in an exoplanet atmosphere". arXiv id not retrieved. — [IPAC record](<https://www.ipac.caltech.edu/publication/2023Natur.617..483T>); [Leiden copy](<https://scholarlypublications.universiteitleiden.nl/access/item%3A3716657/view>); [St Andrews](<https://research-portal.st-andrews.ac.uk/en/publications/direct-evidence-of-photochemistry-in-an-exoplanet-atmosphere/>)
- WASP-107b: see section 3 (methane depletion, hot interior, Ohmic vs tidal explanations). — [astrobiology.com](<https://astrobiology.com/2024/05/20/webb-studies-inflated-exoplanet-wasp-107-b/>); [arXiv 2505.01581](<https://arxiv.org/html/2505.01581v1>)
- Kepler-51d JWST spectrum: featureless, sloped (haze or ring). — [arXiv 2505.21358](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.21358>)

### Inferences (section 8)

- Classification should add axes beyond Teq: metallicity (WASP-39b), internal heat (WASP-107b), haze/cloud state (Kepler-51d).

### Gaps (section 8)

- Recall-based leads (unverified): WASP-39b about 0.28 M_J, about 1.27 R_J, P about 4.05 d (Faedi et al. 2011); JWST ERS Collaboration (2023) CO2 detection, Rustamkulov et al. (2023), Alderson et al. (2023), Ahrer et al. (2023); Dyrek et al. (2024). None checked.
