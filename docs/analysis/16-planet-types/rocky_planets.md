# Rocky / terrestrial planet types (Solar System and exoplanets)

Verification legend. "Verified" = arXiv id, title, authors, DOI checked this session through the arXiv API or a web search result. "Recall" = from training knowledge, NOT checked this session; ids and numbers must be re-checked before publication. All numbers in Recall blocks are approximate.

## Q1. Which rocky planet classes are recognised, and what physically defines each?

### Takeaway (section 1)

The literature recognises compositional classes (Earth-like, iron-rich "super-Mercury", coreless, carbon, water-rich/ocean) and environmental or regime classes (lava/magma-ocean USP, desert/land, runaway-greenhouse Venus-like, snowball/eyeball, stagnant-lid vs plate tectonics). Compositional classes are mostly degenerate in mass and radius alone. Only Earth-like, iron-rich, and hot lava/USP rocks have observational support. Carbon planets, coreless planets, eyeballs, and a confirmed water world remain unconfirmed.

### Cited Findings (section 1)

Compositional classes

- Seager, Kuchner, Hier-Majumder 2007, "Mass-Radius Relationships for Solid Exoplanets", ApJ, DOI 10.1086/521346, arXiv:0707.2895. The standard reference for solid-planet M-R curves (Fe, silicate, ice, etc.). — [arXiv:0707.2895](<https://arxiv.org/abs/0707.2895>)
- Zeng, Sasselov, Jacobsen 2016, "Mass-Radius Relation for Rocky Planets based on PREM", ApJ 819, 127, DOI 10.3847/0004-637X/819/2/127. Uses the Earth's PREM structure to define M-R curves for rocky planets of varied iron fraction. — [arXiv:1512.08827](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.08827>)
- Zeng, Jacobsen, Sasselov et al. 2019, "Growth Model Interpretation of Planet Size Distribution", PNAS 116, 9723, DOI 10.1073/pnas.1812905116. Reproduces the radius valley with a population of water-rich planets plus rocky planets. — [arXiv:1906.04253](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.04253>)
- Dorn, Khan, Heng 2015, "Can we constrain interior structure of rocky exoplanets from mass and radius measurements?", A&A, DOI 10.1051/0004-6361/201424915. M and R alone leave a large degeneracy in interior structure. — [arXiv:1502.03605](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.03605>)
- Otegi, Bouchy, Helled 2020, "Revisited Mass-Radius relations for exoplanets below 120 Earth masses", A&A 634, A43, DOI 10.1051/0004-6361/201936482. Empirical M-R relations separating rocky from volatile-rich populations. — [arXiv:1911.04745](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.04745>)
- Iron-rich / "super-Mercury": Santerne et al. 2018, "An Earth-sized exoplanet with a Mercury-like composition", Nature Astronomy, DOI 10.1038/s41550-018-0420-5. K2-229 b: R = 1.165 ± 0.066 R_E, M = 2.59 ± 0.43 M_E, core-mass fraction near Mercury's (~70% for Mercury versus ~30% for Earth). Explanations offered: giant impact, mantle evaporation, silicate depletion at the inner disk edge. A Wikipedia-sourced core fraction of ~68% was not checked against the paper. — [arXiv:1805.08405](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.08405>)
- Adibekyan et al. 2021, "A compositional link between rocky exoplanets and their host stars", Science 374, 330, DOI 10.1126/science.abg8794. Planet iron-mass fraction correlates with the host star's but is not 1:1 (slope > 4). Super-Earths and super-Mercuries appear as distinct populations, and all five super-Mercuries orbit iron-rich stars. Sample sizes differ across press accounts (22 rocky planets in one). — [arXiv:2102.12444](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.12444>)
- GJ 367 b as an "exo-Mercury": Goffo et al. 2023 give M = 0.633 ± 0.050 M_E, R = 0.699 ± 0.024 R_E, density 10.2 ± 1.3 g/cm3, iron core mass fraction ~0.91 (+0.07, -0.23). A 2026 revisit (Lee et al., arXiv:2606.18355, accepted to ApJ, preprint) finds R = 0.736 ± 0.035 R_E and M = 0.503 ± 0.078 M_E (density 6.9 +1.6/-1.4 g/cm3), iron fraction ~50-70%, i.e. Mercury-like and not the extreme iron-rich case. Treat the "densest USP planet" claim as superseded. — [arXiv:2606.18355](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18355>) (via [search summary](<https://astrobiology.com/2026/06/22/revisiting-the-exo-mercury-candidate-gj-367-b-with-espresso-and-a-self-consistent-tidal-distortion-model/>))
- Kepler-107 b/c "different twins": Bonomo et al. 2019, Nature Astronomy, DOI 10.1038/s41550-018-0684-9. Two similar-size planets with very different densities, with a giant impact (collisional stripping) as the likely origin of the denser one. — [arXiv:1902.01316](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.01316>)
- Coreless: Elkins-Tanton & Seager 2008, "Coreless Terrestrial Exoplanets", ApJ, DOI 10.1086/592316. Purely theoretical: planets formed with oxidised iron (no metallic core) would be nearly indistinguishable from Earth-like in M-R. A 2025 paper (Huang & Dorn, arXiv:2511.01231, "Limits on forming coreless terrestrial worlds in the TRAPPIST-1 system") studies formation limits. Only the title was retrieved. — [arXiv:0808.1908](<https://arxiv.org/abs/0808.1908>); [arXiv:2511.01231](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.01231>)
- Carbon planets: Kuchner & Seager 2005, "Extrasolar Carbon Planets", arXiv:astro-ph/0504214 (no journal ref returned; the work is a preprint-only entry in the API). Theoretical planets formed in C/O > 1 disks (SiC, graphite/diamond, carbide interiors). — [arXiv:astro-ph/0504214](<https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0504214>)
- Madhusudhan, Lee, Mousis 2012, "A Possible Carbon-rich Interior in Super-Earth 55 Cancri e", ApJL 759, L40, DOI 10.1088/2041-8205/759/2/L40. This was the one observational claim for a carbon planet. It rested on the host star's C/O > 1 and the planet's M-R. — [arXiv:1210.2720](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1210.2720>)
- Ocean planets: Léger, Selsis, Sotin et al. 2004, "A New Family of Planets? 'Ocean Planets'", Icarus 169, 499, DOI 10.1016/j.icarus.2004.01.001. Planets formed beyond the snow line that migrated inward; hot or ice-VII mantle layer depending on temperature. — [arXiv:astro-ph/0308324](<https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0308324>)
- Luque & Pallé 2022, "Density, not radius, separates rocky and water-rich small planets orbiting M dwarf stars", Science, DOI 10.1126/science.abl7164. 43 planets (R < 4 R_E) around 26 M dwarfs in three density groups: rocky (~1.0 Earth density), water-rich (~0.5, modelled as ~50% rock/50% water, with water largely in the interior and not a surface ocean) and puffy gas-rich (~0.24). A formation scenario with migration from beyond the snow line is proposed. Co-author Bean noted "smoking gun" proof of a water world is still wanted. — [arXiv:2209.03871](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.03871>); summary [astrobiology.com](<https://astrobiology.com/2022/09/08/density-not-radius-separates-rocky-and-water-rich-small-planets-orbiting-m-dwarf-stars/>)
- Water-world candidate papers found in the arXiv search: TOI-544 b (Osborne et al. 2023, [arXiv:2310.14908](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.14908>)), Ross 176 b (Geraldía-González et al. 2025, A&A, DOI 10.1051/0004-6361/202553719, [arXiv:2507.15763](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.15763>)), TOI-238 b and TOI-1685 b (Egger et al. 2025, [arXiv:2502.07887](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07887>)). All are candidates with degenerate interiors.

Hot rock / lava / ultra-short-period (USP)

- Sanchis-Ojeda, Rappaport, Winn et al. 2014, "A Study of the Shortest-Period Planets Found With Kepler", ApJ 787, 47, DOI 10.1088/0004-637X/787/1/47. The USP population, defined as P < 1 day. — [arXiv:1403.2379](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.2379>)
- Winn, Sanchis-Ojeda, Rappaport 2018, "Kepler-78 and the Ultra-Short-Period Planets", New Astronomy Reviews 83, 37, DOI 10.1016/j.newar.2019.03.006. The review of USP properties and origins. — [arXiv:1803.03303](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.03303>)
- CoRoT-7 b: mass estimates are contested (~4.8 M_E initially; later ~6.06 ± 0.65 M_E commonly cited; 8 M_E in one paper). R = 1.58 ± 0.10 R_E, temperature range cited 1300-1800 K, with the substellar point hot enough to melt and distil silicates into a lava ocean. Density figures range from 5.6 to ~10 g/cm3 depending on the mass adopted, so do not quote a single value. — [arXiv:1105.3372 On the Mass of CoRoT-7b](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/1105.3372>); [arXiv:1102.1629](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/1102.1629>); [Wikipedia summary](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CoRoT-7b>) (secondary)
- Kepler-10 b: M = 4.56 ± 1.23 M_E, R = 1.416 ± 0.033 R_E, density 8.8 ± 2.5 g/cm3, between Earth-like and Mercury-like. — [arXiv:1103.3725](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/1103.3725>) (search result; the figures are from the search summary of the literature, not from a check of the paper)
- K2-141 b: Malavolta et al. 2018, AJ, DOI 10.3847/1538-3881/aaa5b5, "An ultra-short period rocky super-Earth with a secondary eclipse and a Neptune-like companion around K2-141". About R = 1.51 R_E, M = 5.08 M_E, density ~8.2 g/cm3 (search summary, secondary). A temperature of 4599 ± 79 K appeared unlabelled in the search results; do not use it without checking. — [arXiv:1801.03502](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.03502>)
- 55 Cnc e, JWST: Hu et al. 2024, "A secondary atmosphere on the rocky exoplanet 55 Cancri e", Nature 630, 609, DOI 10.1038/s41586-024-07432-x. NIRCam and MIRI thermal emission (4-12 micron) rules out a bare lava world with tenuous rock vapour. It indicates a volatile atmosphere likely rich in CO2 or CO, sustained by magma-ocean outgassing. Press accounts report a best match of CO2, CO and N2 and no H/He. Gases were not directly detected. — [arXiv:2405.04744](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.04744>)
- LHS 3844 b, bare rock: Kreidberg et al. 2019, "Absence of a thick atmosphere on the terrestrial exoplanet LHS 3844b", Nature, DOI 10.1038/s41586-019-1497-4. Spitzer phase curve, no thick atmosphere. — [arXiv:1908.06834](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.06834>)
- TOI-561 b, JWST: ApJL, 11 Dec 2025, "A Thick Volatile Atmosphere on the Ultrahot Super-Earth TOI-561 b". R ~1.4 R_E, M ~3.2 M_E, density ~5.5 g/cm3, P < 11 h. NIRSpec 3-5 micron dayside is inconsistent with a bare rock. Models with water-rich atmospheres fit better. The authors say a global magma ocean may sit below it and that the mechanism of retention is unknown. — [arXiv:2509.17231](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.17231>); [Groningen summary](<https://www.rug.nl/fse/news/matter-and-space/james-webb-space-telescope-detects-thick-atmosphere-around-broiling-lava-world?lang=en>)
- TRAPPIST-1 b: Greene et al. 2023, "Thermal emission from the Earth-sized exoplanet TRAPPIST-1 b using JWST", Nature 618, 39, DOI 10.1038/s41586-023-05951-7. Dayside emission constrains a thick atmosphere (a rocky, likely bare or thin-atmosphere planet). — [arXiv:2303.14849](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.14849>)
- TRAPPIST-1 c: Zieba et al. 2023, "No thick carbon dioxide atmosphere on the rocky exoplanet TRAPPIST-1 c", Nature, DOI 10.1038/s41586-023-06232-z. — [arXiv:2306.10150](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.10150>)
- JWST Rocky Worlds DDT programme: GJ 3929 b is likely a bare rock, with CO2-rich atmospheres thicker than ~100 mbar ruled out at >3 sigma (first results). A June 2026 four-eclipse analysis leaves room for a thin outgassed atmosphere. LHS 1140 b observations are in progress with no verdict found; the LTT 1445 A planets have no published verdict found. Nine targets in total. These are mostly preprints. — [arXiv:2508.12516](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.12516>); [arXiv:2606.07511](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07511>); [STScI Rocky Worlds](<https://archive.stsci.edu/hlsp/rocky-worlds>)

Desert / land planets and tectonic regimes

- Abe, Abe-Ouchi, Sakamoto, Sasamori 2011, "Habitable Zone Limits for Dry Planets", Astrobiology 11(5), DOI 10.1089/ast.2010.0545. Dry "land planets" have wider habitable zones than "aqua planets". Freezing occurred at 77% of Earth's insolation for a low-obliquity land planet versus 90% for an aqua planet. The runaway threshold for the land planet is quoted as 415 W/m2 (~0.77 AU) in one source and 375 W/m2 in another, so check the paper. The authors suggest Venus may have been a habitable land planet until ~1 Gyr ago. — [VPL entry](<https://vpl.uw.edu/publication/habitable-zone-limits-for-dry-planets-astrobiology-2011>); [NASA Astrobiology news](<https://astrobiology.nasa.gov/news/forget-watery-worlds-look-for-life-on-dune-planets/>)
- Plate tectonics on super-Earths: Valencia & O'Connell 2007 (with Sasselov), "Inevitability of Plate Tectonics on Super-Earths", ApJL 670, L45, DOI 10.1086/524012 argue plate tectonics becomes more likely with mass. O'Neill & Lenardic 2007, "Geological consequences of super-sized Earths", GRL 34, L19204, DOI 10.1029/2007GL030598 argue the opposite (stagnant or episodic lid). Stamenkovic et al. 2010 and Noack & Breuer 2011 side with the decreasing trend. Valencia et al. 2011 (EPSL) found equal likelihood for internally heated convection. Unresolved. — [arXiv:0710.0699](<https://arxiv.org/abs/0710.0699>); search summary at [Super-Earths review](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/1108.0031>)
- Eyeball-to-snowball transition driven by sea-ice drift on tidally locked planets: Yang & Ji 2019, [arXiv:1912.11377](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.11377>) (title only retrieved).

### Inferences (section 1)

- The classes form two axes, composition (Fe/silicate/C/H2O, set at formation) and state or regime (set by irradiation, rotation, volatile inventory, tectonics). Real planets sit at a combination, e.g. a "hot iron-rich USP" like K2-229 b or a "water-rich M-dwarf planet".
- With R and M alone, coreless, Earth-like, and in part ocean-world compositions are not distinguishable (Dorn 2015; Elkins-Tanton & Seager 2008). Host-star abundances and atmospheric spectra are the extra constraints.
- Observational status: Earth-like to Mercury-like bulk density, USP lava planets, and bare-rock surfaces are observed. Volatile atmospheres on hot rocks are now seen (55 Cnc e, TOI-561 b), which cuts against the old "bare lava world" picture. Carbon, coreless, desert, snowball and eyeball planets have no confirmed exoplanet example. A water-world is only a candidate.

## Q2. Which mass-radius-composition relations separate the classes?

### Takeaway (section 2)

Zeng et al. 2016 (PREM-based), Seager et al. 2007, and Dorn et al. 2015 provide the M-R curves: for a given mass, pure iron gives the smallest radius and water or ice the largest, with Earth-like rock between. M-R alone is degenerate. Host-star composition (Adibekyan 2021), density, and atmospheric data break the degeneracy.

### Cited Findings (section 2)

- Seager et al. 2007 give M-R curves for single-material solid planets (iron, silicate, ice, carbon compounds) and mixtures. — [arXiv:0707.2895](<https://arxiv.org/abs/0707.2895>)
- Zeng, Sasselov, Jacobsen 2016: PREM-based M-R relation for rocky planets with varying iron fraction. — [arXiv:1512.08827](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.08827>)
- Zeng et al. 2019: the water-rich versus rocky population as a function of size (radius valley interpretation). — [arXiv:1906.04253](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.04253>)
- Dorn et al. 2015: the interior (core size, mantle composition) is largely degenerate given M and R, and the posterior depends on priors. — [arXiv:1502.03605](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.03605>)
- Otegi et al. 2020 gives empirical M-R relations splitting rocky from volatile-rich planets below 120 M_E. — [arXiv:1911.04745](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.04745>)
- Luque & Pallé 2022: density, not radius, separates rocky from water-rich M-dwarf planets. — [arXiv:2209.03871](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.03871>)
- Adibekyan 2021: iron-mass fraction of rocky planets correlates with host-star iron-to-silicate. — [arXiv:2102.12444](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.12444>)

### Recall (unverified) (section 2)

- Zeng 2016 power laws (approximate): Earth-like (32.5% Fe by mass) R ~ M^0.27 R_E for M up to ~8 M_E; pure iron R ~ 0.77 M^0.25; pure MgSiO3 ~ 1.07 M^0.27; 50% water ~ 1.2-1.3 M^0.27 (recheck coefficients).
- Rough bulk-density bands (g/cm3, recall): iron-rich > 9 (Mercury-like at low mass), Earth-like ~5-6 at 1 M_E scaling upward with compression, water-rich ~3-4, volatile envelope < 3.
- Equilibrium temperature has no direct role in the M-R curves; it matters for state classes: lava worlds Teq > ~1500-2000 K (surface melting; CoRoT-7 b 1300-1800 K quoted above), USP P < 1 d (Sanchis-Ojeda 2014), habitable zone inner edge ~0.97-0.99 AU in solar terms (Kopparapu 2013).

### Inferences (section 2)

- A usable catalogue entry should list a density band plus an independent diagnostic (stellar Fe/Mg/Si, atmosphere, or tidal Love number), not a bare radius.

### Gaps (section 2)

- No verified numeric coefficients for the Zeng or Seager fits. A report writer should fetch Table 3 of Zeng 2016.

## Q3. Upper mass limit for rocky planets; transition to sub-Neptunes

### Takeaway (section 3)

There is no sharp mass limit. Radius-based statistical studies place the rock-to-volatile transition at about 1.5-1.6 R_E (Weiss & Marcy 2014; Rogers 2015). Chen & Kipping 2017 define the "Terran" regime statistically with a transition at ~2 M_E that is not a composition boundary. Individual planets above this can still be rocky (e.g. 55 Cnc e at ~8 M_E, 1.9 R_E).

### Cited Findings (section 3)

- Rogers 2015, "Most 1.6 Earth-Radius Planets are not Rocky", ApJ 801, 41, DOI 10.1088/0004-637X/801/1/41: above ~1.6 R_E most planets are not rocky. — [arXiv:1407.4457](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1407.4457>)
- Chen & Kipping 2017, "Probabilistic Forecasting of the Masses and Radii of Other Worlds", ApJ 834, 17, DOI 10.3847/1538-4357/834/1/17. A probabilistic M-R forecast with Terran, Neptunian, Jovian and stellar regimes. — [arXiv:1603.08614](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.08614>)
- Otegi et al. 2020 (above) offers empirical rocky/volatile separation. — [arXiv:1911.04745](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.04745>)
- Zeng 2019 growth model for the radius valley. — [arXiv:1906.04253](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.04253>)
- Bonomo et al. 2019: two planets of similar size and mass but different density in Kepler-107, caused by a collision. — [arXiv:1902.01316](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.01316>)

### Recall (unverified) (section 3)

- Chen & Kipping: Terran to Neptunian transition at about 2.0 M_E (-0.6, +0.7), Neptunian to Jovian at ~0.41 M_J (~130 M_E). I believe the paper says R ~ M^0.28 for Terran.
- Weiss & Marcy 2014 (ApJL 783, L6, arXiv:1312.0936): transition around 1.5 R_E, 4.0 M_E. Fulton et al. 2017 (AJ 154, 109, arXiv:1703.10375): radius gap at ~1.5-2.0 R_E. Owen & Wu 2013 (ApJ 775, 105), Lopez & Fulton 2014/2013: photoevaporation explanation. Dressing et al. 2015 (ApJ 800, 135): Earth-like composition to ~6 M_E. Critical core mass for runaway gas accretion about 10 M_E (Pollack 1996, Mizuno 1980).

### Inferences (section 3)

- A planner could treat rocky as R < ~1.6 R_E and M < ~6 M_E as the typical regime, with an overlap zone to ~2 R_E and ~10 M_E where density decides, and with post-formation processes (photoevaporation, collisions) able to leave a rock above the cut.

### Gaps (section 3)

- No verified mass limit from planet-formation theory. Recall values need checking.

## Q4. Confirmed versus theoretical classes

### Takeaway (section 4)

Confirmed or well-supported: Earth-like, Mercury-like iron-rich (K2-229 b; GJ 367 b weakened by the 2026 revision), lava/USP planets (CoRoT-7 b, K2-141 b, 55 Cnc e, LHS 3844 b, TOI-561 b), and volatile-atmosphere hot rocks (JWST). Purely theoretical or unconfirmed: carbon planets (the 55 Cnc e claim is weakened), coreless planets, desert planets, snowball/eyeball planets, and confirmed ocean/water worlds (candidates only). Tectonic regime is known for Solar System bodies only.

### Cited Findings (section 4)

- 55 Cnc e atmosphere (JWST) implies a CO2/CO-rich volatile atmosphere, contrary to a pure rock-vapour lava world and not obviously carbon-planet interior. — [arXiv:2405.04744](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.04744>)
- LHS 3844 b is a likely bare rock. — [arXiv:1908.06834](<https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.06834>)
- TOI-561 b hosts a thick volatile atmosphere. — [arXiv:2509.17231](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.17231>)
- GJ 3929 b is likely bare. — [arXiv:2508.12516](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.12516>)
- TRAPPIST-1 b/c have no thick atmospheres. — [arXiv:2303.14849](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.14849>), [arXiv:2306.10150](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.10150>)
- GJ 367 b revised to a Mercury-like (not extreme iron) planet in 2026. — [arXiv:2606.18355](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18355>)
- Water-rich classes remain candidates ([TOI-544 b](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.14908>), [Ross 176 b](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.15763>), [TOI-238 b and TOI-1685 b](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07887>)); Luque & Pallé state the water is not necessarily at the surface. — [arXiv:2209.03871](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.03871>)
- The tectonic regime question is unresolved for super-Earths. — [arXiv:0710.0699](<https://arxiv.org/abs/0710.0699>) and [search summary](<https://arxiv.org/pdf/1108.0031>)

### Inferences (section 4)

- A cosmic-shoreline picture (an empirical determination, [arXiv:2508.12865](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.12865>), title only retrieved) frames which hot rocks keep atmospheres. The 2025-2026 results (TOI-561 b thick, GJ 3929 b bare) show the boundary is not simple.
- For a map or world generator, plausible classes are: Earth-like, iron-rich (Mercury-like), lava/USP (with or without volatile atmosphere), ocean/water-rich, desert/land, Venus-like runaway greenhouse, snowball, eyeball (tidally locked), and the unconfirmed theoretical ones (carbon, coreless), with stagnant-lid vs plate-tectonic as a modifier.
