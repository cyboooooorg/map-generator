# General review, 2026-11-04

A review of the whole application after the work on stars, giant planets,
moons and their skies, made by the two sessions that built them:

- **Moons, skies and moon views:** the session that built them.
- **The rest:** this session, with three read-only review agents, one per area. The areas are the
  generator (stars, giants, exomoons, requests), the server, API and CLI,
  and the web app (star view, giant planets, world card, form).

Every finding was read in the code; those marked *to check* could not be
confirmed without running a specific case. Severities: **Major** (wrong
results or lost work a user can hit), **Minor** (small defects, edge
cases, drift), **Suggestion** (quality or design).

State at the time of the review: `main` at `de06a2b`, deployed on
<http://planets.localhost>. All tests pass:

| server   | 34    |
| web      | 184   |

`clippy -D warnings` and `svelte-check` are clean. Nothing in this report
is critical.

## Generator (Rust): stars, giants, exomoons, requests

No critical bug. The physics checks out: the Kopparapu fit, Kepler, Hill
spacing, Heppenheimer's forced eccentricity, Peale's tidal heating, and
the `M^−0.31` inverse-CDF sampling. Each subsystem draws from its own
seeded stream (`GIANTS`, `HOST`, `METALLICITY`, `GIANT_LOOK`), so existing
seeds keep their worlds. Performance is fine: the giant, host and system
draws run a handful of times per world, never per pixel or per tile.

## Moons, skies and moon views

*Reviewed by the session that built them.*

### Works

- Moon systems: impact, debris and capture channels, Roche and Hill
  stability, chaotic-zone spacing, requested counts honoured (fewer than
  1 % of systems redrawn); each moon a generated world in `moons/<n>/`,
  irregular ones with a shape; giants' regular moons from their disk.
- Skies: planet, moons, giant and siblings at their true size (at least
  about 2.5 px), lit with their phases, mapped, shaded as spheres (#137),
  in front of the stars and the star, casting eclipses (up to 4 casters);
  the host planet's own switches; no seam bar.
- The orbit slider and its animation move the moons, their phases and
  eclipses; on a moon the sun follows the orbit and the stars turn with
  it (#136); orbit lines (#139) and labels (#138) in the sky.

### Limitations and suggestions

| #   | Finding                                                                                   | Suggested fix                                                    | Fixed             |
| --- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------- |
| 1   | The planet does not spin in its moon's sky (its map is fixed in the moon's frame).        | Turn its longitude by the planet's day over time.                | #141              |
| 2   | Moon phases at time 0 are a display choice (moon k placed 2.4·k rad past opposition).     | Document it.                                                     | #145 (documented) |
| 3   | Orbits are circular in the sky: eccentricity ignored in positions, sizes and orbit lines. | Use Kepler positions.                                            | #145              |
| 4   | Sky maps turn about the spin axis only: an inclined or retrograde moon lacks its roll.    | Apply its axis' tilt.                                            | #141              |
| 5   | Moons a few km across are 2.5 px dots, like stars.                                        | The labels help; a faint ring around sub-pixel bodies would too. | #145              |
| 6   | Eclipses keep the 4 largest casters; small moons' shadows drop in 6-moon systems.         | Harmless; document.                                              | #145 (documented) |
| 7   | The orbit time is neither saved nor in the view link, and carries over between worlds.    | Reset it on a world change; add it to the link.                  | #143              |
| 8   | A giant's siblings are plain disks, not generated worlds.                                 | Generate them as moons.                                          | #156              |
| 9   | On a moon, the sun's latitude uses the moon's own season and tilt.                        | Approximate for inclined moons; derive it from the planet's.     | #155              |

Tests: `lib/skyBodies.test.ts` covers directions, faces, the moon-system
sky, the sun on a moon, halos and orbit loops. The shaders are checked by
hand in the browser only.

## Strengths worth keeping

- **Reproducibility:** one seeded stream per subsystem, new values always
  drawn after the existing ones, so a seed keeps its world across
  versions.
- **Backward compatibility:** older `world.json` formats load, with
  `serde(default)` and explicit fallbacks (the Sun at 1 AU, no giants
  modelled, the Solar System's crater rate).
- **Robust server:** strict ids, temporary directories, cancellable jobs,
  revision-keyed caches.
- **Science with sources:** every model cites its paper in the code and
  in `docs/analysis/`, and statistical tests check the rates against the
  observations they come from.
- **Typed bilingual UI:** the English and French catalogs are checked
  against each other, and a test refuses text written in the templates.
